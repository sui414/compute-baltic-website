A pricing language for compute and tokens: spot, margin, forward.

AI compute is the largest new commodity market in the world, and it trades without a common price language: the same GPU-hour is worth wildly different amounts depending on the workload it runs, the interconnect it sits on, the service level it promises, and the venue that quotes it. The Compute Baltic exists to make that market legible — daily benchmarks, margin models, and research built from primary data collected around the clock.

Why "Baltic"

The name honors the maritime benchmark markets, where the price of the last great non-storable commodity — ship-days — has been assessed daily for centuries. An unused GPU-hour evaporates exactly the way an unused ship-day does; and like freight, compute will not collapse onto a single world price. Shipping solved this with route-standardized assessments that the whole market settles to. Compute needs the same, and this desk is our attempt to build it: the Baltic Composite Index (CBI) and its family of sub-benchmarks. We are an independent research effort — not affiliated with any exchange.

Data Catalog

Compute MarginOpen now. The full P&L of a unit of compute: what each accelerator costs to buy or rent, the token revenue it can generate at settled market prices, and the margins left for each business model — owner, landlord, operator.
GPU IndexThe rental-price layer: CBI and its per-chip sub-indices, cross-venue composites with dispersion bands, marketplace microstructure, and the know-your-silicon reference.
Token IndexThe output layer: what a million tokens sells for — per frontier lab and per open model — plus usage volumes and cache economics from the routing layer's daily record.
Provider LandscapeWho serves what on which hardware, at what implied margin — per-endpoint silicon inference and price forensics across the serving market.
Liquidity CurveWhether you can actually get the compute: order-book depth, stock levels, and the term structure from one week to a year.
Model CompatibilityWhich models run on which silicon — marketplace recommendations, memory arithmetic, and observed production deployments, three independent lenses.
