Methodology

How the numbers on this desk are collected, computed, and refreshed. Independent research; not affiliated with any exchange. Contact: danning@panteracapital.com · @sui414.

The index family

  • CBI — Baltic Composite Index (methodology v1.2, 2026-08-14). One number for the price of standardized GPU compute, in index points. Base = 1000 on 2026-01-28.
    • Venue level: each family's daily value is the median of $/GPU-hr on-demand rates across reporting venues — robust to venues with thin books posting outlier quotes.
    • Family level: constituents combine by fleet-share weight — each family's 30-day trailing share of listed GPUs across venues that publish counts — with a 10% floor for admitted families without count data, renormalized daily. Current weights: H100 24% · H200 37% · B200 36%.
    • Admission rule (mechanical, no discretion): a chip family joins when quoted on ≥2 independent venues for 14 consecutive days; it enters at half weight for its first 30 days, then full. It exits after 30 days below two-venue coverage. Founding constituents (H100, H200, B200) grandfathered at base. B300 is currently a candidate — priced on a single venue, published as a sub-index but excluded from the composite until a second venue reports.
    • Rebasing: each family rebased at its first assessment; version changes are announced here with effect dates.
  • CBI-<chip> — the quotable dollar price: cross-venue median rental rate, USD per GPU-hour, on-demand, per family.
  • CBTY — Token Yield. Dollars of tokens one B200 prints per hour: benchmark throughput × settled token prices; median model, ≥30 tok/s per user, sold-out basis (a gross-revenue ceiling).
  • CBTS — Token Spread. (Token yield − rental rate) ÷ token yield on the same chip — the operator's gross conversion margin.

Data sources

  • GPU rental rates: vast.ai marketplace (our own 5-minute order-book sweeps; historical daily series from Jan 28, 2026), ORNN curated index (from Apr 2026), RunPod (posted + reserved term structure), Silicon Data headline values (display with attribution), Prime Intellect, Mithril.
  • Posted list prices: CoreWeave (instance-normalized), Nebius, Crusoe; AI principals — Fireworks, Together, Modal (per-second normalized), Baseten (per-minute normalized).
  • Token prices & usage: OpenRouter — token-weighted effective prices (post-cache, measured across live traffic), per-model daily volumes, cache-hit rates; ORNN per-lab token indices.
  • Inference physics: SemiAnalysis InferenceX open benchmark (Apache-2.0) — tokens/sec per GPU (input/output split, normalized over the full prefill+decode pool) × interactivity, 11 open frontier models × 10 accelerators, with dated history since Sep 2025.
  • Hardware prices: public street-quote ranges (OEM/analyst coverage, evidence-labeled), one formal OEM quote (fully configured, labeled), and Compute Exchange secondary-market prints (used/refurbished, daily) for market-implied depreciation.
  • Venues & derivatives watch: Itô Compute basket NAVs, Kalshi compute series, Architect, ComputeIndex, USD.ai financing metrics.

Collection cadence & refresh schedule

  • Every 5 minutes (within hourly windows): vast.ai order-book sweeps; OpenRouter performance percentiles.
  • Daily 15:00 UTC: full collection — every OpenRouter model × provider endpoint, ORNN, RunPod, Kalshi, InferenceX, venue stack, secondary hardware market.
  • Daily 16:45 UTC: the site republishes — indices recomputed, every chart regenerated. The banner's "latest data" date reflects the last completed cycle.

Model conventions

  • Ceiling vs realized. "Ceiling" figures assume sold-out 24/7 at quoted prices. "Realized" figures apply the operating chain: 95% online × 97% SLA pass × 70% paid utilization (an assumption — no public source measures it) × 85% share-and-price realization − $0.25/GPU-hr power + colo.
  • Cache handling. Prices are measured token-weighted effective prices, so observed caching is already in the settle; no separate cache multiplier (it would double-count).
  • Hyperscaler and spot venues (added 2026-08-24). Azure via its official retail prices API — the richest source on the desk, returning every SKU × region × tier, so on-demand, spot, Low Priority and reserved arrive together (H100 NC40ads_H100_v5 in East US: on-demand $6.98, spot $1.29, Low Priority $1.40). AWS via the public Spot advisor feed, spot only — its on-demand list is published elsewhere and is deliberately not inferred from it. Only single-GPU SKUs (NC40ads, p5.4xlarge) are used in cross-venue comparisons, because they need no bundling divisor. Region dispersion is large — Azure H100 spot spans roughly $1.29–$2.18 across 25 regions — so the cheapest region is quoted and named. Spheron is collected but currently yields nothing: its rate table is client-rendered, and its prices are on-demand rather than spot despite being commonly cited as spot.
  • Bundled specs are NOT normalized. Every $/GPU-hr on this desk divides a price that may also buy vCPU, RAM and local storage, and those bundles differ wildly between SKUs — Mithril’s H100 instance carries 12.5TB of NVMe against 1.1TB on its H200, which alone moves the per-GPU figure from $4.81 to $1.70. We do not adjust for this, because only Mithril and CoreWeave publish the bundle; ORNN, Silicon Data, SemiAnalysis, Prime Intellect, SF Compute and the serving platforms publish nothing, and subtracting an imputed storage cost would require a $/GB-hr price we would have to invent. So the composite, the ledger’s Rent column, the crack spread and the break-even clock all inherit this: they compare prices for goods that are similar, not identical. Read the cross-venue band as the width of that ambiguity, not as error around a true price.
  • Per-GPU basis. All per-GPU figures divide by the full serving pool (including prefill GPUs); NVL72 rack systems divide by 72 on both cost and throughput.
  • Evidence labels. Street estimate ≠ formal quote ≠ landed cost; thin-market rack rents are indications, not executable quotes; depreciation is market-implied only where secondary prints exist.

Caveats

  • Benchmarks cover open models only; medians are menu statistics, not volume weights.
  • Serving software improves monthly — the physics layer itself drifts upward.
  • Street prices move; ranges are point-in-time and evidence-labeled.

Data inventory & venue set (v1.2)

Everything the desk collects, and what role it plays in the index. The composite uses market venues only; reference quotes are tracked and displayed but excluded by design.

Fills versus asks — the asymmetry to read every margin through. The revenue leg of the margin is settled: OpenRouter effective prices are what buyers were actually billed on live traffic, cache included. The cost leg is almost entirely quoted — venue asks and posted rate cards, not cleared trades. Only SF Compute runs an order book and prints cleared prices, and it clears H100 roughly 30% below our five-venue composite. Taken alone, a quoted cost leg understates the token margin. But the revenue leg is biased the other way and by more, because it is a sold-out ceiling — so the two errors oppose, and only the rent one is currently measurable. Read every margin as a bracket, and read the break-even utilization column as the number that tells you how much revenue-side error the trade can absorb.

A second consequence of a quote-based cost leg: the quotes do not move. Across the neocloud and inference-principal rate cards we track, 0 of 43 vendor-SKU series changed price over eight days of daily observation — which is why they are collected but kept out of the composite.

Specs are not matched — but the contract type is. A composite “H100” blends SXM and PCIe parts, vetted-datacenter and marketplace-host tiers, and every geography; none of the venues publishes interconnect, and region is country-level at best. The cross-venue band is therefore not error around a true price — it is the absence of a single underlying.

The one dimension we do hold fixed is the product: every series in the composite is on-demand hourly. GPU rates are ordered by delivery promise — an interruptible or preemptible hour prices below a guaranteed on-demand hour, and a committed reserved term prices below both — so blending them would drag the median down and read as a cheaper market rather than a different product. Collected but excluded on those grounds: RunPod’s reserved rates (1wk/1mo/3mo/6mo), vast.ai’s bid/interruptible book, Prime Intellect’s spot and community tiers, Mithril’s spot instances. Two imperfections remain: ORNN and Silicon Data publish a single daily index number without documenting its term, so both are assumed on-demand; and SF Compute’s clearing average pools every duration traded that day, its contracts being arbitrary-length.
  • Index venues, active: vast.ai (order-book sweeps at 5-minute resolution + feed history, from 2026-01-28) · ORNN (transacted index) · RunPod (posted on-demand, daily from 2026-07-28) · SF Compute (daily order-book clearing average, from 2026-07-25 — the only cleared price in the venue set; H100 prints most days, H200/B200 list but rarely trade).
  • Index venues, ramping: Silicon Data (daily H100/H200/B200 index prints, collected from 2026-08-11 — the same index family CME’s October futures settle on) · Prime Intellect (posted on-demand per-GPU, from 2026-08-11). A venue joins a family’s median mechanically after 14 reporting days within a 16-day window; both are on track to admit in late August. Until then they appear in the coverage lanes but not the median.
  • Tracked, excluded from the index by design: posted list prices from neoclouds (CoreWeave, Nebius, Crusoe) and AI principals (Fireworks, Together, Modal, Baseten), collected daily from 2026-08-12 — rate-card asks are sticky and rarely paid at size; including them would bias the median upward and deaden the index. Mithril (spot/interruptible 7-day averages) — a different firmness of delivery than the on-demand hour the index prices. Chutes (decentralized marketplace) — monitored, thin. Empirically, the exclusion is justified by stickiness rather than by level: none of the 43 tracked vendor-SKU rate-card series moved across eight days of daily observation.
  • Reference series (not spot inputs): CME × Silicon Data compute futures — announced August 11 (the launch the financialization commentary covers); no market data exists until trading opens October 5, at which point daily settlement prints will be collected as the market’s first term structure · SemiAnalysis quarterly H100 1-yr contract index · Kalshi GPU prediction ladders · Itô compute baskets (NAVs) · Architect/AX listed compute futures — dated H100/H200/B200/B300 contracts (Sep & Dec 2026, 730 GPU-hr multiplier) live since 2026-08-13, the first listed GPU term structure; specs snapshotted daily, marks gated behind exchange auth · Compute Exchange secondary hardware quotes (capital-asset prices, not rentals) · USD.ai financing yields.

Version history

  • v1.3 (2026-08-24) — SF Compute admitted, the venue set’s first cleared-price source (order-book daily clearing average; H100 in practice). Both legs of the margin now share one trailing 7-day window, with rents averaged per venue before the cross-venue median so a venue’s reporting calendar cannot move the level. Composite day universe now spans every venue rather than vast alone, and a venue’s last print carries forward at most 3 days — a stalled vast collector had frozen all rents for six days under a current as-of stamp. Mithril removed from the ledger rent to match this page (it had been included there while excluded here). Prefill/decode throughputs rescaled onto the full installed pool, which lowers rack-scale revenue materially (~3× on GB200/GB300, where ~90% of benchmarked configs are disaggregated).
  • v1.2 (2026-08-17) — venue set expanded: Silicon Data and Prime Intellect added with a mechanical 14-of-16-day venue admission rule; list-price and spot quotes documented as tracked-but-excluded. No level change on adoption date (both venues still ramping).
  • v1.1 (2026-08-14) — fleet-share weighting, mechanical family admission, B300 candidate status.
  • v1.0 (2026-08-13) — launch: equal-weight cross-venue medians, base 1000 on 2026-01-28.
